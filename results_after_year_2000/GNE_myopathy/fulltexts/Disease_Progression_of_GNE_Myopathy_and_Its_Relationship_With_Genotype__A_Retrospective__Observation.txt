GNE myopathy, the most common distal myopathy in China,1 is a rare autosomal recessive skeletal muscle disorder characterized by weakness in the tibial anterior muscle, abnormal walking posture, and foot drop.2,3 Generally, the disease progresses slowly in young patients, initially affecting the lower extremities, followed by the involvement of other muscles, eventually resulting in the disability of independent ambulation loss and wheelchair dependency.4,5

To date, more than 250 pathogenic and likely pathogenic variants in the GNE gene that cause GNE myopathy have been identified.6,7 The spectrum of GNE gene mutations exhibits ethnic differences. For example, p.V603L is the most common mutation observed in patients from Japan,8 p.M743T in patients from the Middle East,9 and p.V727M in patients from Southeast Asia.10 Multiple cohort studies have revealed the relationships between various mutations and disease severity among patients with GNE myopathy.11-14 In Asia, the p.D207V mutation has been reported as the most common and second most common mutation of patients with GNE myopathy in China and Japan, respectively,1,15-17 and patients with the p.D207V mutation exhibited a later age at onset.15,17 Although the clinical and pathologic characterization of Chinese patients with GNE myopathy has been reported in several previous studies,16,18 studies on the natural disease progression of detailed motor dysfunction are rare. In addition, the relationship between the p.D207V hotspot mutation and clinical phenotype remains unclear.

In this study, we retrospectively investigated the motor function involvement during disease progression among patients with GNE myopathy based on a Chinese single-center cohort. In addition, we explored the relationship between disease severity and genotype in patients with GNE myopathy by analyzing the activities of daily living (ADL) and the onset age of motor function involvement.

This retrospective and observational cohort study was conducted to investigate the motor function involvement during disease progression in patients with genetically confirmed GNE myopathy. All participants who were diagnosed with GNE myopathy, between 2000 and 2023 from the Peking University First Hospital in China, were included in this study.18 Patients with GNE myopathy were included according to the following criteria as previously described5,18-20: (1) preferential weakness of the tibial anterior muscle with relatively selective sparing of the quadriceps in early-stage disease and (2) genetic testing revealing pathogenic biallelic mutations in the GNE gene. Patients who lacked detailed information on GNE mutations or were lost to follow-up for motor function assessments or had impaired motor function due to trauma were excluded. This project was approved by the ethics committee of Peking University First Hospital, and informed consent was obtained from all participants.

The genetic tests of GNE gene variation were performed in all patients. GNE pathogenic variants were collected from medical records. In brief, mutations in the GNE gene were detected by next-generation sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Detailed information on GNE gene mutations among the patients with GNE myopathy included in this study is shown in eTable 1. Patients with GNE myopathy were divided into 2 subgroups based on whether they harbored the hotspot mutation of p.D207V: (1) the group with the p.D207V mutation (n = 40) and (2) the group without the p.D207V mutation (n = 43).

Clinical assessments of patients with GNE myopathy were performed at study initiation and follow-up through outpatient services or telephone surveys performed by the same neurologist. Follow-up assessments were continued until December 2023. Clinical data collected included detailed phenotypes, comorbid diseases (including hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, transient thrombocytopenia, and thrombocytopenia), age at onset (AAO), initial presentation symptoms, onset ages of different clinically significant muscular problems, and disease progression (mainly obtained from patients' records or recollections).

Symptom onset was defined as the first appearance of muscle weakness symptoms; accordingly, AAO was defined as the age at the occurrence of first-ever muscle weakness. Patients with GNE myopathy were divided into 2 subgroups based on the median AAO: (1) the group with an earlier AAO (≤26 years) (n = 42) and (2) the group with a later AAO (>26 years) (n = 41).

The clinically significant muscular problems associated with disease progression were systematically collected to evaluate the motor function according to the GNE Myopathy Functional Activity Scale (GNEM-FAS).20,21 In brief, motor function involvement, including lower extremity weakness (including foot drop, unable to run, unable to climb stairs, independent ambulation loss, and wheelchair dependency) and upper extremity weakness (including difficulty in opening bottles, difficulty in making fist, and difficulty in lifting the upper extremity), was assessed.

Specifically, the independent ambulation loss, leaving participants unable to walk without assistance, was strictly based on the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score with a grade greater than or equal to 4 (mRS ≥4).22,23 Wheelchair dependency was defined based on an mRS score of grades 4–5, indicating wheelchair use for most activities or being wheelchair-bound for most of the day.

The Barthel indexes (BIs) were collected to assess current activity abilities.24 Patients with GNE with BIs <60 were indicated to have functional dependency.25 The Spearman rank correlation coefficient was used to examine the correlations between age at onset, disease duration, age at examination, and the BI score. In addition, a multivariate linear regression was fitted, including sex, age at onset, and disease duration as variables, to further assess the association of the BI score with age at onset and disease duration. The regression coefficient beta (β), its corresponding p value, and the explained variance (R2) were reported.

For the natural history analysis of patients with GNE myopathy, continuous variables are presented as the median (range) or mean (SD) and categorical data are presented as frequency and percentage (%). To investigate the natural history differences between patients with GNE myopathy with and without the hotspot mutation p.D207V, comparisons of nonparametric continuous variables were conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using a χ2 test (with the Fisher exact test used when the expected value was <5).

Kaplan-Meier curves were used to estimate the median onset age for each clinically significant muscular problem, along with the corresponding 95% CIs. Log-rank tests were used to analyze the statistical disparities in symptom onset, independent ambulation loss, wheelchair dependency, and functional dependency among GNE myopathy subgroups during the follow-up period. Multivariate Cox models (adjusted for sex [categorical variable] and disease duration [continuous variable]) were implemented to obtain adjusted hazard ratios (aHRs) and 95% CIs for patients without the p.D207V mutation compared with the patients with the p.D207V mutation for the outcomes of symptom onset, independent ambulation loss, and wheelchair dependency.

All the statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 22 and GraphPad Prism 8 software. p < 0.05 was defined to be statistically significant.

At the time the data were collected, a total of 146 participants were enrolled. Seventeen participants lacking detailed genetic information and 46 who were lost to follow-up were subsequently excluded, leaving a total of 83 patients with genetically confirmed GNE myopathy included in the analysis. Among the 83 patients with GNE myopathy, 48 (57.8%) were women; the median age at examination of patients with GNE myopathy was 36 years (range 25–57); and the median age at diagnosis was 30 years (range 21–51). Regarding comorbidities, only 9 patients reported transient thrombocytopenia, of whom 2 were prescribed thrombocytopenia (Table 1).

Generally, the median age at onset of patients with GNE myopathy was 26 years (range 16–46). Among these patients, the 3 most common initial symptoms reported were foot drop (33.7%), abnormal walking posture (19.3%), and a propensity for falls (14.5%) (Table 1). In addition, 16 patients (19.3%) reported initial symptoms of lower extremity weakness, including difficulty walking, running, and climbing stairs, and only 4 patients reported lower extremity pain.

To investigate the disease progression of motor function involvement in the natural history of patients with GNE myopathy, we obtained follow-up data on clinically significant muscular problems in the upper and lower extremities. Over a median disease duration of 10 years (range 1–26), 37 patients (44.6%) lost independent ambulation and 27 patients (32.5%) developed wheelchair dependency (Table 1 and eTable 2). For lower extremity involvement, Kaplan-Meier curves showed that foot drop was the earliest common symptom, with a median onset age of 28 years (95% CI 27–29). The subsequently occurring symptoms included unable to run with a median onset age of 28 years (95% CI 27–29), unable to climb stairs at 32 years (95% CI 30–34), independent ambulation loss at 33 years (95% CI 31–35), and wheelchair dependency at 36 years (95% CI 32–40) (Figure 2). In addition, upper extremity impairment occurred relatively later, with difficulty in opening bottles at a median onset age of 31 years (95% CI 30–32), difficulty in making fist at 32 years (95% CI 31–33), and difficulty in lifting the upper extremity at 34 years (95% CI 33–35) (Figure 2). Similar trends were observed in the disease duration for each clinically significant muscular problem (eFigure 1, eTable 2).

The Kaplan-Meier curves illustrate the onset age for each clinically significant muscular problem: foot drop, unable to run, unable to climb stairs, difficulty in opening bottles, difficulty in making fist, difficulty in lifting the upper extremity, independent ambulation loss, and wheelchair dependency, along with corresponding 95% CI.

Furthermore, we investigated the gender differences in the progression of motor function involvement among patients with GNE myopathy (eTable 3). No significant differences were detected between men and women, regarding either onset age or disease duration of each clinically significant muscular problem (all p > 0.05).

Given the high frequency of the p.D207V mutation among patients with GNE myopathy in this study, we next compared the clinically significant muscular problems associated with disease progression between patients with and without the hotspot mutation p.D207V. Compared with the patients without the p.D207V mutation, we observed that patients with the p.D207V mutation had significantly later onset ages for each clinically significant muscular problem (all p < 0.05). However, there were no significant differences in the duration from symptom onset to the occurrence of any clinically significant muscular problem between the 2 subgroups (all p > 0.05) (Table 2).

In this study, we investigated the motor function involvement during disease progression in patients with GNE myopathy from a large Chinese cohort and approximately half of the patients (48.2%) harbored the p.D207V variant. Regarding the relationship between motor function involvement and the genotype of the GNE mutation, we found that patients with the p.D207V mutation not only showed later initial symptom onset, as suggested in previous studies,15,17 but also exhibited a later onset age of clinically significant muscular problems during disease progression. Our study results provide valuable insights into the natural history of motor function among patients with GNE myopathy, enhancing our understanding of the genotype-phenotype association in this condition. These findings have the potential to improve clinical management and guide ongoing clinical trials.19,26,27

Patients with GNE myopathy typically present with symptoms caused by weakness of the tibialis anterior muscle, such as gait disturbance, foot drop, tripping, or inability to lift their toes.7,12-15,21 Our study showed that 33.7% of patients with GNE myopathy initially presented with foot drop while others exhibited an affected walking posture and a propensity for falls due to distal muscle weakness, consistent with previous reports. In addition, the median AAO of patients with GNE myopathy was 26 years (range 16–46), which was similar to that in the Japanese cohort.11,28 Regarding clinically significant muscular problems, we found that the onset age of distal upper extremity weakness, such as difficulties in making a fist and opening bottles, was later than of inability to run. Although studies have indicated that the function of quadriceps is preserved relatively,2-4,7 the muscles of the posterior thigh group are commonly involved. Particularly, we found that the onset of difficulty in lifting the upper extremity and inability to climb stairs was followed shortly by independent ambulation loss, suggesting that muscle weakness of the proximal extremity may be a prognostic indicator of the disease that warrants attention in clinical practice.

In the disease progression of GNE myopathy, the loss of independent ambulation emerged as a pivotal prognostic indicator. In our investigation, we used a collective assessment of walking ability using the mRS and the GNEM-FAS. Through this approach, we determined that the rate of wheelchair dependency among patients with GNE myopathy was 32.5%, which is slightly lower than that reported in other cohorts.20,24 Conventionally, independent walking ability can persist for approximately 5–10 years after disease onset.7,16 However, our cohort displayed considerable variability in the onset of independent ambulation loss and wheelchair dependency, with median disease durations of 8 years (range 3–20) and 10 years (range 6–26), respectively. We conducted a detailed analysis of the influence of gender differences on the disease progression of motor function involvement in patients with GNE myopathy. However, gender differences were not observed in either the onset age or disease duration of any of the clinically significant muscular problems. Thus, the role of genetic heterogeneity needs to be given special consideration.

Among patients with GNE myopathy with compound heterozygous mutations in this study, the missense mutation p.D207V was the most common pathogenic allele (27.4%), with a prevalence slightly higher than the 25.5% reported in another recent study in China.29 Moreover, patients with GNE myopathy with the p.D207V compound heterozygous mutation accounted for approximately half (48.2%) of our cohort, which is close to the 54.1% reported in a recent 10-year follow-up study of Japanese patients with GNE myopathy.28 These findings further indicated that p.D207V is the predominant mutation among Chinese patients with GNE myopathy.

Previous studies have reported that patients harboring the p.D207V mutation exhibit a later AAO, and it has been found in Japanese cohorts that patients with the p.D207V homozygous mutation exhibit milder symptoms.11,28 These findings suggest that the enzymatic change caused by the p.D207V mutation might be mild because of partial defects in oligomerization and product trafficking by the mutated GNE protein, according to the structural features of the GNE enzyme.17 However, a Chinese patient with a p.D207V homozygous mutation exhibited an earlier AAO.16 In this study, we observed that patients with the p.D207V mutation had a disease duration until wheelchair dependency of 9 years, which is much earlier than the 21 years observed in Japanese patients with GNE myopathy with the p.D207V/V603L mutation.28 The potential impact of the p.V603L mutation needs to be considered because there were no patients with the p.V603L compound heterozygous mutation in our cohort. In addition, we found that patients without the p.D207V mutation had a significantly lower risk of progressing to independent ambulation loss and wheelchair dependency, than did those with the p.D207V mutation. Notably, patients with the p.D207V mutation exhibited significantly later onset ages of each clinically significant muscular problem, but there were no differences in the corresponding disease duration. This result indicates that early onset may serve as a potential prognostic marker for patients with GNE myopathy.

The correlation between the number of affected joints and ADL has been confirmed in a Korean cohort.24 Besides, there were no significant changes in BIs in a 5-year observational follow-up study of a Japanese cohort.10 According to our follow-up assessments of ADL using BI scores, multivariate linear regression analysis revealed that an earlier AAO and a longer disease duration were associated with lower BI scores. Furthermore, through stratifying patients with GNE myopathy based on the median AAO, we observed that patients with an earlier AAO developed functional dependency within a shorter disease duration, than did those with a later AAO. These findings further indicate that early onset is a potential poor prognostic indicator; therefore, early onset should be considered when grouping patients in clinical drug trial.19,26 In addition, ADL analysis also confirmed functional dependency differences between patients with and without the p.D207V mutation.

This study had several limitations. First, recall bias regarding the onset age of motor function symptoms from patients' report is inevitable. Therefore, we used both the mRS and GNEM-FAS to identify the key disability events. Second, not all patients were followed up solely through clinical outpatient visits; thus, we lacked quantitative assessment data, such as manual muscle testing scores and quantitative MRI data. This study primarily assessed the development of motor function involvement in patients with GNE myopathy based on the evaluation of clinically significant muscular problems. Finally, because GNE is a distinctively bifunctional enzyme and the position of GNE mutations may influence the phenotype, our analysis focused only on the hotspot mutation of p.D207V without categorizing other structural domain mutations. In the active phase of clinical trials, future research will be based on multicenter collaborations, including longitudinal follow-up and imaging assessments, to further elucidate the genotype-phenotype differences and motor function changes in GNE myopathy.
